Overall Satisfaction

90% of participants rated the program "Excellent" and 10% rated it "Very Good." Across all nine programme quality indicators, every respondent registered positive agreement — a result without a single neutral, negative, or dissenting rating across the entire satisfaction battery.

The evidence strongly supports formalising AU's Executive Education portfolio. Immediate priorities include: (1) designing advanced and sector-specific follow-on programmes; (2) developing customised closed-session offerings for government entities; (3) building applied simulation labs; (4) exploring an accredited diploma pathway; and (5) establishing an executive resource hub as a recurring engagement touchpoint.

Section 02

Program Context

Ajman University delivered its inaugural Executive Education programme under the theme Institutional Reputation Protection in Times of Crisis: Applications in Government Communication and Artificial Intelligence. This programme marked a significant institutional milestone — AU's first deliberate foray into executive-level learning designed for senior government leaders, communications professionals, and institutional decision-makers, rather than for conventional academic audiences.

The programme addressed a set of interconnected, high-stakes competency areas that are acutely relevant to the regional leadership landscape: crisis communication and institutional resilience; the threat environment created by misinformation, disinformation, and deepfakes; AI-enabled reputation monitoring and risk analysis; governance and leadership during crises; and the strategic integration of communication with institutional leadership functions.

From a strategic positioning standpoint, this programme serves multiple purposes simultaneously:

✓
A Successful First Executive Offering

The programme demonstrated AU's capacity to design and deliver high-calibre executive-level content in a format appropriate for senior leaders and decision-makers, with facilitator expertise, peer dialogue, and strategic depth that participants validated.

✓
A Proof of Concept

Participant response validates the executive education model itself — showing that AU can create compelling, strategically relevant experiences that resonate with government executives, generate demand for more, and differentiate AU in the higher education landscape.

✓
A Platform for Portfolio Expansion

The feedback provides both evidence and momentum to build a curated executive education portfolio — one that encompasses AI governance, crisis readiness, reputation management, government communication, and leadership capability development.

✓
A Partnership Development Vehicle

The feedback suggests potential interest in customized organizational offerings, particularly among government-sector participants, which may be worth exploring through future engagement.

Theme 1 — Interactive Discussions & Peer Exchange

The programme's participatory format was its most universally praised characteristic. Nine of ten respondents selected "Interactive discussions" and "Peer knowledge exchange" as primary value-added elements. Participants described the quality of dialogue among senior leaders as a distinctive experience that cannot be replicated in conventional lecture formats.

"The interactive discussions and exchange of expertise among participants [were the standout elements]."
— Iman · Programme Participant
"Interactive discussions, peer exchange, AI in crisis management, and strategic insights [were the most valuable components]."
— Dr. Raida · Programme Participant

Theme 2 — AI in Reputation & Crisis Management

This was the only element selected by all ten participants (100%). The consensus signals that AI-related content is not merely of interest but perceived as central to executive competency in the current environment. Multiple respondents went further to articulate specific AI applications they wished to explore — particularly the use of AI tools for government indicator formulation, strategic communication, and media monitoring.

"The strategic application of AI in crisis communication ecosystems [is the priority topic for future programmes]."
— Jawahir Al-Naqbi · Programme Participant
"How to use AI tools in formulating and analysing government indicators [should be covered in future sessions]."
— Saud Sultan Al-Shamri · Programme Participant

Theme 3 — Practical Cases & Applicable Frameworks

Six respondents valued real-world case studies and four specifically cited practical tools and frameworks. Participants expressed a strong preference for applied learning over conceptual exposition. One note of feedback — that a COVID-related presentation was perceived as less engaging — suggests that case material requires careful calibration for relevance to the specific audience context.

Theme 4 — Demand for Advanced & Customised Programmes

The qualitative responses reveal a strong appetite for building upon this foundation. Respondents articulated a layered demand hierarchy: advanced specialised programmes, organisation-specific customised programmes, closed executive sessions, AI and decision-making programmes, and communities of professional practice. One respondent requested conversion to a formal accredited diploma pathway.

"The programme should be converted to an accredited diploma."
— Jawahir Al-Naqbi · Programme Participant
"[We need] programmes that are longer and continuous."
— Iman · Programme Participant

Theme 5 — Government-Sector Specificity

Two respondents explicitly raised the need for government-sector examples. One participant observed that since all attendees represented government entities, the use of examples drawn exclusively from private-sector or international contexts was a missed opportunity for contextual resonance.

"Since the attendees are from government entities, we need real-world examples from the government sector."
— Nasser Abdullah · Programme Participant

Priority Topics for Future Executive Programmes

Respondents identified several thematic priorities for future offerings:

  • Crisis decision-making under pressure
  • Strategic planning, smart media monitoring, and simulation
  • Psychological operations and information warfare
  • AI for government indicator analysis and strategic communication
  • Geopolitical crisis management
  • Combating misinformation and rumours during operational crises
  • Cybersecurity threats to institutional reputation
  • Integration of AI, crisis management, and cybersecurity teams
  • Official spokesperson readiness and media crisis management
Section 07

Strategic Interpretation

What does this feedback tell AU strategically? The programme was not merely well-received — it validated a market hypothesis. Executive-level participants in the UAE government sector are under-served by existing professional development offerings in the specific domains of AI governance, crisis communication, and reputation management. AU entered this space and was immediately recognised as competent, credible, and relevant.

Does it validate the executive education model? Yes. The 100% positive rating across all satisfaction items — combined with unprompted suggestions for diplomas, multi-day formats, and follow-on programmes — demonstrates that the model works: peer-level dialogue, applied frameworks, and expert facilitation constitute a compelling executive experience. Participant feedback suggests that interactive dialogue, peer exchange, and applied discussion were among the most valued aspects of the experience.

Market demand signals. The demand for customised programmes is not speculative — eight of ten participants explicitly confirmed it. Their organisations represent government communications, aviation safety, public security, and strategic intelligence functions. These are well-resourced institutions with senior leadership development mandates and the authority to commission bespoke programmes. AU should view these participants as both a validation cohort and an initial client pipeline.

AI governance and crisis readiness as strategic pillars. The unanimous selection of AI-in-crisis-management as a valued element, combined with the priority topics identified for future programmes, reveals a clear market positioning opportunity. The intersection of AI governance, crisis communication, and institutional reputation appears to offer a distinctive thematic space for AU's emerging Executive Education work.

From event to portfolio. The path from a single programme to a scalable executive education portfolio is well-lit by this data: advanced versions of the current programme; sector-specific editions (aviation, public security, government communications); an AI decision-making module; a crisis simulation lab; a practitioner community; and potentially an accredited diploma. Each of these is grounded in participant-expressed demand, not assumption.

Section 09

Areas for Enhancement

The following areas for development are grounded entirely in participant feedback and are framed as strategic opportunities rather than deficiencies. They reflect the ambitions of an already satisfied cohort seeking more.

Programme Duration & Continuity

Multiple participants explicitly requested multi-day formats and ongoing programme continuity. The depth and richness of the dialogue appears to have created appetite that a single-day session could not fully satisfy. Opportunity: Design a two- to three-day advanced edition, or a modular series across quarterly sessions.

Government-Sector Case Calibration

Given the composition of the cohort (predominantly government entities), participants requested examples drawn from the public sector. One case study (COVID-related) was perceived as less engaging. Opportunity: Curate a government-sector case library covering UAE, GCC, and regional crisis communication scenarios.

Applied Simulation Components

Participants requested applied simulation labs and practical case work beyond conceptual discussion. The demand for "applied labs and case studies" featured in 40% of responses. Opportunity: Develop tabletop crisis simulation exercises as a dedicated programme module.

Accredited Pathways

At least one respondent called for conversion to an accredited diploma. This signals institutional seriousness about the programme's value. Opportunity: Explore a professional certificate or executive diploma designation in partnership with AU's academic governance structures.

Content Balance: Depth vs. Coverage

While the balance between conceptual and practical was rated highly, the existence of one less-engaging presentation segment suggests content variety and pacing deserve deliberate attention in future iterations. Opportunity: Review content sequencing and apply peer-based facilitation models more extensively throughout.

Post-Programme Engagement Structures

Six respondents expressed interest in communities of practice and ongoing knowledge exchange. The programme currently ends at delivery. Opportunity: Design a structured alumni network and follow-up engagement model to extend the programme's value beyond the event itself.

Section 11

Recommendations

1

Continue and Scale AU Executive Education

This inaugural programme has generated the evidence base to justify formalising executive education as an institutional priority. Establish a dedicated executive education function within AU — with its own branding, budget, and delivery calendar — to sustain and grow this market position.

2

Develop an Advanced Edition of this Programme

Design a two- to three-day successor programme with deeper simulation components, government-sector case studies, and expanded AI modules. Build on the same facilitator credibility and dialogue-centred format, while addressing the calls for longer duration and applied learning.

3

Offer Customised Programmes for Government & Institutional Partners

Establish a client development process to convert programme participants into institutional clients. Eight respondents explicitly confirmed their organisations need customised programmes. Prioritise outreach to participants from the aviation, public security, and strategic communications sectors.

4

Build Executive Simulation-Based Learning Formats

Develop crisis simulation labs as a flagship format — immersive, team-based, and directly applicable to institutional contexts. This format addresses the strongest gap identified in the feedback and differentiates AU from academic training providers.

5

Create a Curated Executive Resource Hub

Establish a digital resource hub for executive alumni — curated readings, AI tools, crisis communication frameworks, and case studies. This extends programme value beyond the event and creates a recurring engagement touchpoint with participants and their organisations.

6

Explore Certificate or Diploma Accreditation Pathways

Assess the feasibility of a modular executive certificate or diploma in Crisis Communication, AI Governance, or Institutional Reputation Management. Participant demand is evidenced; a formal credential adds institutional legitimacy and enables partnership with government training mandates.

7

The Feedback Provides a Positive Narrative for Future Executive Education Communication

This report — and the validated feedback it contains — provides AU with a credible, evidence-based narrative for positioning its executive education capabilities externally. Use the results to engage potential government and corporate partners, and to inform proposals for Executive Education development funding.

Section 12

Conclusion

An Encouraging First Indicator of Executive Relevance and Demand

The evidence assembled in this report tells a clear and confident story. Ajman University's first Executive Education programme was not merely a successful event — it was a validated institutional experiment. Every participant left satisfied. Every satisfaction indicator received unanimous positive endorsement. Eight in ten confirmed their organisations need more. Every single respondent selected AI in crisis management as a valued element. And the programme's facilitator earned unanimous recognition for expertise and credibility.

Taken together, the findings suggest that the inaugural program resonated strongly with its participants and offers useful insight into the kinds of executive learning experiences that may hold relevance for AU’s future Executive Education direction. No other UAE university occupies that intersection with visible commitment.
